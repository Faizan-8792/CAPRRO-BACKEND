Trade Receivables — Long Outstanding Balances [WP Ref: A-11]

Objective
To assess the existence, recoverability and appropriate presentation of trade receivables at year end.

Background / Facts Noted
Trade receivables as at 31 March 2025 amounted to Rs. 9,40,00,000.
An ageing review identified Rs. 1,15,00,000 outstanding from three customers for more than 270 days.
One customer, Apex Engineering Ltd., owed Rs. 48,00,000. The customer disputed Rs. 18,00,000 of this balance in correspondence dated January 2025, stating that certain supplied components were defective.
Management has not recorded any specific provision against the disputed amount.
Subsequent receipts from Apex Engineering between April and June 2025 totalled Rs. 22,00,000.

Audit Procedures Performed
Obtained the receivables ageing report and agreed the total to the general ledger.
Sent external balance confirmations to the three customers.
Examined subsequent receipts through bank statements.
Reviewed correspondence available between the company and Apex Engineering.
Discussed the disputed balance with the Finance Manager.

Findings / Observations
Confirmation responses were received from two of the three customers.
Apex Engineering's confirmation was not returned.
Rs. 22,00,000 was subsequently collected from Apex Engineering.
Management continues to consider the entire remaining balance recoverable.
No documented assessment of the disputed Rs. 18,00,000 has been prepared.

Preparer's Conclusion
Subsequent collections provide some evidence regarding recoverability; however, the disputed amount requires further evaluation before concluding on the adequacy of any expected credit loss provision.

Revenue — Cut-off Around Year End [WP Ref: B-12]

Objective
To determine whether sales are recorded in the appropriate accounting period.

Background / Facts Noted
Revenue for FY 2024-25 was Rs. 18,60,00,000.
During testing, 12 sales invoices dated 29–31 March 2025 were selected, representing total revenue of Rs. 74,00,000.
For four invoices totalling Rs. 31,00,000, the corresponding goods dispatch documents showed that the goods left the company's warehouse between 2 April and 5 April 2025.
The sales invoices had nevertheless been recorded in March 2025.
Management stated that the invoices were raised before year end because customers had "confirmed their orders."

Audit Procedures Performed
Selected year-end sales invoices using a risk-based sample.
Agreed invoice amounts to the sales ledger.
Inspected dispatch documentation for selected transactions.
Tested sales recorded during the first week of April for potential prior-period transactions.
Discussed exceptions with the Sales Controller.

Findings / Observations
Four transactions totalling Rs. 31,00,000 were invoiced and recorded before year end although dispatch occurred after year end.
No evidence was provided showing that control over the goods had transferred to the customers before 31 March 2025.
The transactions were reversed from the sales ledger in April only after the audit team raised the issue.

Preparer's Conclusion
The identified transactions require adjustment to ensure revenue is recognized in the appropriate reporting period. The pattern also warrants consideration in the assessment of revenue cut-off risk.

Payroll — Employee Master File Changes [WP Ref: C-13]

Objective
To assess whether payroll payments are made only to valid employees and whether changes to employee master data are appropriately controlled.

Background / Facts Noted
Monthly payroll averages approximately Rs. 1,35,00,000.
The HR system maintains employee bank-account details and salary information.
During the audit, the team obtained a report of employee master-file changes for March 2025.
The report showed 17 changes to bank account details during the month.
Of these, 11 changes were made by the Payroll Executive using the Payroll Administrator login.
The Payroll Manager stated that the Payroll Administrator was on leave during several of the dates shown in the report.
One employee's bank account was changed on 27 March 2025, and Rs. 3,80,000 was subsequently transferred to the new account.
The employee confirmed that the new bank account belonged to them.

Audit Procedures Performed
Obtained the March payroll master-change report.
Reviewed changes to employee bank details.
Compared selected changes with employee confirmation records.
Discussed access rights with HR and payroll personnel.
Traced selected salary payments to bank statements.

Findings / Observations
Multiple bank-account changes were processed using a login assigned to an employee who was reportedly absent.
No evidence of independent review or approval of the changes was available.
The identified employee confirmed ownership of the new bank account.
No unexplained payroll payment was identified from the sample tested.

Preparer's Conclusion
The absence of evidence of independent review over payroll master-file changes represents a control deficiency. Further investigation is required to determine whether the issue is isolated or indicative of broader unauthorized access.

Inventory — Standard Cost vs. Net Realizable Value [WP Ref: D-14]

Objective
To determine whether inventory is carried at an appropriate amount at year end.

Background / Facts Noted
Finished goods inventory at 31 March 2025 amounted to Rs. 4,80,00,000.
A product-level analysis identified Product X with inventory of Rs. 52,00,000.
The standard cost per unit is Rs. 1,040.
Recent sales invoices for Product X show selling prices ranging between Rs. 980 and Rs. 1,020 per unit.
Management's inventory valuation report continues to value the product at standard cost of Rs. 1,040 per unit.
Management explained that the standard cost includes normal production overheads and that the selling-price reduction is temporary.
No subsequent sales above Rs. 1,040 per unit were identified in the documents provided to the audit team.

Audit Procedures Performed
Obtained the inventory valuation report.
Tested standard costs to the approved costing records.
Reviewed recent sales invoices for selected products.
Compared selling prices with standard costs.
Discussed pricing trends with the Production Manager.

Findings / Observations
Recent selling prices for Product X are consistently below its recorded standard cost.
No formal NRV assessment for Product X was available.
Management has not recorded a write-down against the Rs. 52,00,000 balance.

Preparer's Conclusion
Further work is required to determine whether the carrying amount of Product X exceeds its recoverable selling value after considering estimated costs necessary to complete and sell the inventory.

Bank Reconciliation — Unreconciled Differences [WP Ref: E-15]

Objective
To assess whether bank balances are complete and accurately reconciled at year end.

Background / Facts Noted
The company maintains 14 bank accounts.
Bank reconciliations are prepared monthly by the Treasury Accountant and reviewed by the Finance Manager.
The March 2025 reconciliation for the company's principal operating account showed an unreconciled difference of Rs. 6,70,000.
The difference consists of:
Rs. 2,10,000 of cheques allegedly issued but not cleared for more than six months.
Rs. 1,80,000 described as "bank charges to be confirmed."
Rs. 2,80,000 described as "other reconciling items."
The Finance Manager stated that these balances "usually clear in the following months."

Audit Procedures Performed
Obtained the March bank reconciliation.
Agreed the book balance to the general ledger.
Agreed the bank balance to the external bank statement.
Tested selected outstanding cheques to subsequent bank statements.
Requested explanations and supporting documents for reconciling items.

Findings / Observations
The Rs. 2,10,000 of outstanding cheques remained uncleared as of 30 June 2025.
Supporting documentation for the Rs. 2,80,000 "other reconciling items" was not provided.
The Rs. 1,80,000 bank-charge balance was subsequently identified as charges recorded by the bank but not entered in the company's books.

Preparer's Conclusion
The aged outstanding cheques and unsupported reconciling items require investigation and potential adjustment. The bank reconciliation control should also be evaluated for timely resolution of reconciling differences.

Borrowings — Covenant Compliance [WP Ref: F-16]

Objective
To determine whether borrowings and related covenant compliance have been appropriately assessed and disclosed.

Background / Facts Noted
The company has a term loan of Rs. 12,00,00,000 from Zenith Bank.
The loan agreement requires a minimum current ratio of 1.1:1 and a maximum debt-to-equity ratio of 2.5:1.
At 31 March 2025, management's calculations showed:
Current ratio: 1.08:1
Debt-to-equity ratio: 2.3:1
The bank waiver letter dated 10 April 2025 states that the current-ratio breach for the year ended 31 March 2025 is waived until 30 September 2025.
The draft financial statements classify the entire Rs. 12,00,00,000 borrowing as a non-current liability.
No reference to the covenant breach or waiver is currently included in the financial statement notes.

Audit Procedures Performed
Read the executed loan agreement.
Recalculated covenant ratios using year-end financial information.
Obtained the bank's waiver letter.
Discussed the classification with the Finance Controller.
Reviewed the draft borrowing note.

Findings / Observations
The current-ratio covenant was breached at the reporting date.
A waiver was obtained after year end.
The entire borrowing remains classified as non-current.
No specific disclosure regarding the breach or subsequent waiver is included in the current draft.

Preparer's Conclusion
The accounting and disclosure implications of the covenant breach require further evaluation, including consideration of the timing and terms of the waiver.

Management Override — Unusual Manual Journal Entries [WP Ref: G-17]

Objective
To identify unusual journal entries that may indicate management override or inappropriate financial reporting.

Background / Facts Noted
The audit team extracted all manual journal entries posted during March 2025.
One entry dated 31 March 2025 debited "Miscellaneous Expenses" by Rs. 44,00,000 and credited "Accrued Expenses" by the same amount.
The entry description stated: "Year-end adjustment — management request."
No supporting invoice or third-party documentation was attached.
During discussion, management stated that the amount represented "expected vendor costs relating to the current year."
The underlying vendor invoices were received in April 2025 and totalled Rs. 41,50,000.
The remaining Rs. 2,50,000 has not been supported by documentation.

Audit Procedures Performed
Extracted March manual journal entries.
Identified entries posted above the audit team's journal-testing threshold.
Inspected supporting documentation.
Compared the accrual with invoices subsequently received.
Discussed the entry with the Financial Controller.

Findings / Observations
Rs. 41,50,000 of the accrual was subsequently supported by vendor invoices relating to the current year.
Rs. 2,50,000 remains unsupported.
The journal was posted manually at period end at the request of management.
No evidence of independent review of the journal entry was available.

Preparer's Conclusion
The supported portion appears consistent with a year-end accrual; however, the unsupported Rs. 2,50,000 requires resolution. The circumstances of the manual entry should also be considered in the overall assessment of management override risk.
